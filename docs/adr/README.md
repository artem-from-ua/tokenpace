# Architecture decisions (ADRs)

The project's architecture decision records. Every **accepted** ADR is immutable; a `draft` can be
reworked until it becomes `accepted`. When a decision goes out of date its record stays, and its
number and title are struck through in the index.

**Full vs partial supersession.** We distinguish two cases, and their frontmatter differs:

- **Full** — the whole decision is replaced by a newer ADR. In the frontmatter: `status: superseded` plus `superseded_by: [NNNN]`. In the table, the status reads `superseded`.
- **Partial** — the newer ADR supersedes only a *fragment* of the decision (one of its sections, say, or an option's default), and the rest still stands. Then `status` **stays `accepted`** (the decision mostly still holds — marking it `superseded` would be inaccurate), but `superseded_by: [NNNN]` is added, and a postscript at the top of the ADR's body draws the line between what exactly was superseded and what still stands. In the table, the status is written descriptively: `partially superseded → NNNN (what exactly)`.

In both cases the number and title in the table are struck through. Examples of partial supersession are [0025](0025-check-for-updates.md) and [0033](0033-automatic-update-install.md) (both partially superseded by [0036](0036-update-signals-single-dropdown-item.md)).

**Drafts.** An ADR whose decision is *not* final yet — it is behind a gate (a spike, research, external validation), or it describes a proposed but unimplemented direction — carries `status: draft`. In the table the status reads `draft`; the number and title are **not** struck through (a draft is not out of date). When the decision is made, `draft → accepted` in the same file — and from that moment the ADR is immutable (`draft` is the only status that may be rewritten). A draft that did not take off gets `status: rejected` with a one-line reason. The lifecycle: `draft → accepted → (superseded)`, with a `draft → rejected` branch.
